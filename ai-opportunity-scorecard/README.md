## The outcome

The owner walks out of one session with the whole list in order. Every idea, from every team, has been scored the same way on frequency, friction, input readiness, consequence of error and implementation effort, each 3, 2, 1 or Unknown, and every score shows the fact from the room that earned it. The few at the top are named with the records each would start from and the person who would check its output.

Nothing is guessed. Where nobody knew a fact, the score says Unknown, the total becomes a range, and a Find out line turns the gap into a question with space for who answers and by when. Answering them is how the order changes from one quarter to the next, and a single answer can lift an idea into the first slot.

The scorecard puts the list in order and picks nothing. The leadership team chooses what to build first.

## Who starts the work, and when

The owner, the chief operating officer or the operations lead starts it when the list of ideas is long enough that choosing has stalled, usually fifteen or twenty ideas from across the company. Team leads and the people who do the work sit in the room and supply the facts. Run it again every quarter, with the questions answered and the new ideas added.

## What they supply

- **The opportunity list,** one row per idea: who proposed it, how often the work happens, where it waits or is redone, where its records live and whether they may be used in the company's approved AI workspace, who checks the output before it leaves the team, where the output goes, and which roles and systems it touches.
- **The scoring session:** the team or outcome the list is for, the roles in the room and the date.
- **Changes from the room,** for any score someone disputes, with their reason.
- **Optionally,** last quarter's scorecard and the [workflow inventory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/workflow-inventory/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=workflow-collections&utm_content=leadership-ai-opportunity-scorecard&from=gh-leadership-ai-opportunity-scorecard) of automations already running.

The workflow template works only from what you give it. It connects to no system and never asks for a password or a login code.

## What comes out

One scorecard in a fixed order:

1. **Every idea scored,** each factor with the words from the list that earned it, and a total out of 15 or a range where something is Unknown.
2. **A Find out line for each Unknown,** with who answers and by when left for the room to fill.
3. **The ranked list.**
4. **The top of the list:** the two or three ideas with every score known and a named reviewer, each with what it starts from.
5. **Answer first:** ideas whose range could reach the top once a question is answered.
6. **Waiting for a reviewer:** ideas whose output would go to a customer or into a payment with nobody checking it first.
7. **What the score leaves to the people:** strategy, the pilot, value and next quarter's rescoring, followed by a blank line for the leadership team's decision.

It estimates no value, saving or return, and says nothing about what an idea is worth. Any value claim in the list is quoted and left out of the scores.

## What has to change for your company

Agree your column names before the session, and who is in the room. Settle which AI workspace is approved and who says whether a set of records may be used in it, because that one answer decides many input readiness scores. Decide whether the top of the list is two places or three. Check the readings for the factors, especially where frequency and implementation effort drop to 1, and change them before scoring, never during it. Decide what counts as leaving the team: a report to the board or a lender may count. Keep each quarter's scorecard so the next one shows what moved.
