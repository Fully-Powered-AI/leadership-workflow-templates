# Executive situation brief

Before a conversation that matters, with a key partner, a major customer or a co-founder, the executive gets one brief from the records they approve: the short version in a sentence, what is already decided and promised, what each side has said, the question to settle and the choices open. The executive decides.

*Workflow template · Leadership · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/executive-situation-brief/)*

## The outcome

You walk into the conversation holding the whole record, not your memory of it. One sentence tells you where things stand. Below it: how you got here, every decision already taken and every promise either side made, with whether it was kept, and what the other side has said in its own words, dated, so you can see which positions are fresh and which are months old.

The brief puts the question you need to settle at the centre and lists the choices actually on the table, each with what the records say it would involve and whether agreeing to it on the day would cut across a decision your team has already made. Making no commitment is always one of them.

Where two records disagree, you see both before the other side points it out. Where something needs your lawyer or your accountant, it is listed for them with no view taken. Every line names the record it came from. The brief takes no position, recommends nothing and says nothing about what anyone is like: what you offer, ask for or hold back is your call.

## Who starts the work, and when

The executive starts it, or someone who prepares for them: a chief of staff, an executive assistant, or a founder's right hand. They run it one to three working days before the conversation or the decision, once the records are gathered, and again if something new arrives before the day.

It suits the moments where the history matters: a partner asking for more territory, a major customer asking for new terms (the executive's brief; an account manager's call plan for that customer is sales call preparation), a co-founder wanting to talk about who does what (pay, equity and employment terms stay out), a supplier wanting to change how the two companies work together.

## What they supply

- **The request:** the subject, the other side, the date, who attends, and the question to settle in the executive's own words, if they have one.
- **The approved records:** the emails, meeting notes, summary of the terms you work under, figures and earlier notes the executive is happy to have used. Drafts are set aside.
- **The decision log,** if the company keeps one, as an export.

No mailbox, calendar or customer system is connected, and nothing is looked up on the internet. Everything is pasted or attached by a person.

## What comes out

1. **The short version,** in one sentence.
2. **How you got here,** dated, oldest first.
3. **What is already decided and promised,** each decision with who approved it and whether it is still in force, each promise with whether it was kept.
4. **What each side has said,** in their words, with older positions marked.
5. **Where the records disagree,** both versions side by side.
6. **The question to settle.**
7. **The choices open,** unranked, with making no commitment always among them.
8. **The dates that matter,** counted from the conversation.
9. **Questions for your advisers,** with no view given.

Every gap comes first, as a question with the name of who can answer it.

## What has to change for your company

Decide who prepares the brief for the executive and which records it may draw on, and who approves a record before it is used. Name who owns each kind of record, so a disagreement between two of them reaches the right person. Set how old a stated position may be before it is marked as old, and how many history entries, statements and choices the brief holds. Map your decision log's column headings to the defaults, or keep them; a log kept with the [decision log](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/decision-log/) workflow template exports in the expected shape. Name your advisers. Decide whether the brief ever goes beyond the executive. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, from the records you paste or attach.

1. Read [EXAMPLE.md](EXAMPLE.md) to see what a good result looks like.
2. Open [TEMPLATE.md](TEMPLATE.md) and set every item under "What to customize" to your own rules, roles and limits.
3. Give your AI assistant the instruction block and the output format from the template, with your own limits in place of the ones the instructions leave open in square brackets. Paste them in as custom instructions in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, or copy this whole folder into a product that loads `SKILL.md` packages. This folder stands alone; you do not need the rest of the repository.
