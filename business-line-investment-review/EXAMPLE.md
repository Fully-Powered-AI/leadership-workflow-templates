# Business line investment review: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* The company, the people, the business lines and every figure below are invented. Company R is an invented building-services group of about 90 people with four business lines. Its finance lead prepares the review for the leadership team's session on where to put money and attention in 2027. People are referred to by role. First come the inputs, in full, then the review the capability returned.

## The review

```
BUSINESS LINE INVESTMENT REVIEW: Company R, 12 months to 30 Sep 2026 against 12 months to 30 Sep 2025

For: leadership team, session on Thursday 3 December 2026, to place each line and settle the open questions. Prepared by the finance lead on Tuesday 10 November 2026.
Sources: review settings, finance lead; priorities, managing director's statement of 2 November 2026; performance file and forecasts, finance lead, 6 November 2026; shared-resource file, operations manager, 4 November 2026; owner notes, Head of service, Head of projects, Head of training and Rental manager, 2 to 5 November 2026.
Settings used: amounts in US dollars, in thousands; decline threshold 20%. Defaults used: none.

THE PICTURE
Lines: 4. Latest period finance-reviewed: 3 of 4 (Equipment rental, prior period only).
Placements for discussion: invest 1, investigate 2, reduce commitment 1.
Untested estimates: 2. Accounting differences: 2. Open questions: 8.

BASIS
Periods: all four lines have a row for both periods. Rows not finance-reviewed: Equipment rental, 12 months to 30 Sep 2026.
Tie-out: result equals revenue less direct costs in all 8 rows. Resource shares: 3 of 4 resources add to 100; Sales team adds to 90 (question 7).
Amounts are as supplied, in US dollars, in thousands. Nothing has been adjusted to a common accounting basis (see Accounting differences).

SIDE BY SIDE
L1 Maintenance contracts (Head of service).
Fit: P1 Direct ("led by maintenance contracts and equipment rental"); P2 Owner's claim ("Rental customers often become maintenance customers"); P3 None stated.
Recorded, 12 months to 30 Sep 2026 (finance-reviewed): revenue $3,520k; direct costs $2,816k; result $704k; margin 20.0%.
Recorded, 12 months to 30 Sep 2025 (finance-reviewed): revenue $3,200k; direct costs $2,560k; result $640k; margin 20.0%.
Change: revenue +10.0%; result +10.0%.
Capital used $2,200k; return on capital used 32.0%.
Forecast, 12 months to 30 Sep 2027 (finance-reviewed): revenue $3,850k; result $780k. Shown as supplied; not used in the placement.
Uses: Field technicians 50%; Workshop and yard 30%; Booking and invoicing system 40%; Sales team 45%.
Owner's account (as supplied, not checked): contracts renew on 1 July each year and the renewal rate has held; three technicians planned for next year.

L2 Installation projects (Head of projects).
Fit: P1 None stated; P2 None stated; P3 None stated.
Recorded, 12 months to 30 Sep 2026 (finance-reviewed): revenue $3,800k; direct costs $3,420k; result $380k; margin 10.0%.
Recorded, 12 months to 30 Sep 2025 (finance-reviewed): revenue $4,100k; direct costs $3,485k; result $615k; margin 15.0%.
Change: revenue -7.3%; result -38.2%.
Capital used $1,900k; return on capital used 20.0%.
Forecast, 12 months to 30 Sep 2027 (finance-reviewed): revenue $3,900k; result $430k. Shown as supplied; not used in the placement.
Uses: Field technicians 40%; Workshop and yard 30%; Booking and invoicing system 25%; Sales team 35%.
Owner's account (as supplied, not checked): two signed projects slipped from this year into next year, which the owner says explains most of the fall in revenue.

L3 Training courses (Head of training).
Fit: P1 None stated; P2 Owner's claim ("Training sales lead to installation orders"); P3 None stated.
Recorded, 12 months to 30 Sep 2026 (finance-reviewed): revenue $840k; direct costs $882k; result -$42k; margin -5.0%.
Recorded, 12 months to 30 Sep 2025 (finance-reviewed): revenue $900k; direct costs $810k; result $90k; margin 10.0%.
Change: revenue -6.7%; result down $132k (from $90k to -$42k).
Capital used $300k; return on capital used -14.0%.
Forecast, 12 months to 30 Sep 2027 (finance-reviewed): revenue $800k; result -$60k. Shown as supplied; not used in the placement.
Uses: Field technicians 10%; Booking and invoicing system 15%; Sales team 5%.
Owner's account (as supplied, not checked): courses are how customers learn the company's systems; a mandatory course changed in March and attendance fell.

L4 Equipment rental (Rental manager).
Fit: P1 Direct ("led by maintenance contracts and equipment rental"); P2 None stated; P3 None stated.
Recorded, 12 months to 30 Sep 2026 (NOT finance-reviewed): revenue $1,640k; direct costs $1,148k; result $492k. No measure worked out.
Recorded, 12 months to 30 Sep 2025 (finance-reviewed): revenue $1,500k; direct costs $1,050k; result $450k; margin 30.0%.
Change: not worked out, no reviewed latest period.
Capital used $1,700k (12 months to 30 Sep 2025, the latest reviewed row); return on capital used 26.5%.
Forecast, 12 months to 30 Sep 2027 (NOT finance-reviewed): revenue $1,750k; result $520k. Shown as supplied; not used in the placement.
Uses: Workshop and yard 40%; Booking and invoicing system 20%; Sales team 5%.
Owner's account (as supplied, not checked): the 12 months to 30 Sep 2026 figures are before year-end adjustments and finance has not reviewed them.

SHARED RESOURCES
Field technicians (people): Maintenance contracts 50%, Installation projects 40%, Training courses 10%. Adds to 100.
Workshop and yard (premises): Maintenance contracts 30%, Installation projects 30%, Equipment rental 40%. Adds to 100.
Booking and invoicing system (software): Maintenance contracts 40%, Installation projects 25%, Training courses 15%, Equipment rental 20%. Adds to 100.
Sales team (people): Maintenance contracts 45%, Installation projects 35%, Training courses 5%, Equipment rental 5%. Adds to 90, not 100 (question 7).
No effect on cost or capacity of reducing any line is estimated here.

UNTESTED ESTIMATES
U1. Head of service, L1: about 20% of new maintenance contracts start with rental customers. No contract data supplied. Not used in any measure or placement (question 1).
U2. Head of training, L3: training sales lead to installation orders worth about $300k a year. No order data supplied. Not used in any measure or placement (question 4).

ACCOUNTING DIFFERENCES
A1. Revenue timing: Installation projects recognise revenue on project milestones; Maintenance contracts and Equipment rental monthly; Training courses when a course is delivered. Figures are shown as supplied (question 8).
A2. Training courses: trainer pay is held in central costs, so direct costs of $882k and $810k exclude it. The amount is not supplied (question 5).

PLACEMENT FOR DISCUSSION
L1 Maintenance contracts: Invest. Rule R1 met: a Direct fit (P1); both periods finance-reviewed; latest reviewed result $704k, above zero; result up 10.0% on the prior period, within the 20% decline threshold.
L2 Installation projects: Investigate. R1 not met: no Direct fit, and the result fell 38.2%, more than the 20% threshold. R3 not met: the result is above zero. What would change it: a Direct fit that leadership confirms, and a fall of no more than 20%.
L3 Training courses: Reduce commitment. Rule R3 met: no Direct fit, and the latest reviewed result is below zero (-$42k).
L4 Equipment rental: Investigate. R1 not met: a Direct fit (P1) and a reviewed result above zero ($450k, 12 months to 30 Sep 2025), but only the prior period is reviewed, so the two periods cannot be compared. R3 not met: a Direct fit exists and the result is above zero. What would change it: finance review of the 12 months to 30 Sep 2026 (question 6).
Reduce commitment means lowering the budget, staffing or attention a line gets. The review makes no valuation, no sale or closure recommendation and no forecast. Leadership at Company R decides each placement.

OPEN QUESTIONS
1. For the Head of service (L1): you estimate that about 20% of new contracts start with rental customers. What contract records show this?
2. For the Head of projects (L2): the result fell from $615k to $380k (-38.2%) and you say two signed projects slipped into next year. Which projects, with what values and dates?
3. For the finance lead (L2): does the reviewed forecast of $3,900k include the two slipped projects?
4. For the Head of training (L3): you say training sales lead to installation orders worth about $300k a year. Which orders, and where are they recorded?
5. For the finance lead (L3): what was trainer pay in the 12 months to 30 Sep 2026 and the 12 months to 30 Sep 2025, so that Training courses' direct costs can be shown with it?
6. For the finance lead (L4): when will the Equipment rental figures for the 12 months to 30 Sep 2026 (revenue $1,640k, result $492k) and the forecast for the 12 months to 30 Sep 2027 (revenue $1,750k, result $520k) be reviewed?
7. For the finance lead (all lines): the Sales team shares add to 90. Which line or use makes up the other 10?
8. For the finance lead (all lines): Installation projects recognise revenue on milestones and the other lines monthly or on delivery. Can the 12 months to 30 Sep 2026 be restated on one basis, or can you confirm the difference does not change the comparison?

CHECKS FOR THE FINANCE LEAD
Rows not finance-reviewed: Equipment rental, 12 months to 30 Sep 2026.
Rows where result differs from revenue less direct costs: none.
Resources whose shares do not add to 100: Sales team, 90.
Forecasts not finance-reviewed: Equipment rental.
Lines missing a row for either period: none.
Capital not stated: none.

Draft for the finance lead's review and the leadership team's discussion. Every figure traces to the files named above, dated 2 to 6 November 2026. No figure has been adjusted, no placement is a decision, and nothing has been approved, sent or changed in any record.
```
