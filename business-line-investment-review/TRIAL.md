# Business line investment review: a trial on the invented example

- **Product:** Claude, run in Claude Code
- **Model:** Sonnet 5.5 (model ID claude-sonnet-5-5)
- **Date:** 2026-11-10

I did not write this template. I ran it once on the inputs of its invented example (Company R, a building-services group with four business lines). No client data was used. I read the template and the example's inputs first, produced the review, and only then compared it with the example's published output.

## Inputs used

The five inputs in `public/example.md`, section "The inputs": the review settings, the performance file, the forecasts, the shared-resource file and the four owner notes. Nothing else was supplied.

## What came out

The assistant ran to the end without stopping. All eight rows tied out, and every header and line name matched. Every section of the output format was produced. The full output follows.

## What was observed

Every required section was produced, in the order the format gives. All the figures, margins, changes and returns on capital matched the example's output. The four placements matched too: Maintenance contracts invest, Installation projects and Equipment rental investigate, Training courses reduce commitment. Nothing in the run made the assistant stop. The case of a line whose latest row is not finance-reviewed was handled by the written steps, with Equipment rental placed by the investigate rule.

Differences from the published example:

- Untested estimates: I listed four, the example lists two. Beyond the two both outputs list (the 20% of new contracts from rental customers, and the $300k of installation orders from training), I also listed the three technicians planned for next year and the claim that two slipped projects explain most of the fall in revenue. The example treats those two as the owner's account only.
- Open questions: I wrote nine, the example seven, because of those two extra estimates and because I split the Installation projects question between the head of projects and the finance lead.
- I put the Equipment rental latest row first, as the format's layout shows, and labelled it not reviewed. The example puts the reviewed prior row first. I showed the unreviewed row's capital (1,800) and no measure from it.
- I wrote amounts as plain numbers with the unit stated once ("US dollars, in thousands"). The example writes them as "$3,520k".
- I put the 20% estimate in the Maintenance contracts owner's account, marked as an estimate. The example leaves it out of that line's account and gives it only under untested estimates.
- The example's sources line adds "and managing director, 2 and 10 November 2026" for the settings. Mine names the settings as the finance lead's and the priorities as the managing director's of 2 November, which is what the inputs say.

Facts not in the inputs:

- In my output: in the Installation projects placement I wrote an extra clause that the line "would go to Reduce commitment if the result fell below zero and no Direct fit were found", and a question asking how much of the revenue fall the slippage accounts for "under milestone recognition". Both go a little beyond the inputs. The first is a speculation the template does not ask for; the second assumes the slipped projects affect revenue through milestones, which the inputs do not say.
- In the example's output: "Capital used $1,700k at 30 Sep 2025" gives a point in time; the inputs give capital for a 12-month period only. The example's sources line dates the settings to "10 November", which is the preparation date, not a supply date.

Where the instructions were ambiguous and I had to guess:

1. The definition of an untested estimate covers "any ratio, amount or benefit an owner gives about the lines working together or about the future". The planned technicians and the slipped projects are future-facing owner statements with an amount in them, so I listed them; the example did not. The template does not say whether a plan or a past-cause explanation counts.
2. Fit readings: the template does not say whether the owner's note that counts is only the line's own owner's, or any owner's. I used only the line's own owner's note, so the Head of service's remark about rental customers became an owner's claim for Maintenance contracts and was not applied to Equipment rental.
3. Whether the training owner's remark that training sales lead to installation orders connects to P2 ("win more of the work from customers we already have") is a judgment. I read it as an owner's claim; the priority statement itself names no line, so it can never be Direct.
4. For Equipment rental, the rule R1 asks for "a prior reviewed period". The only reviewed row is the prior period, so the line has a latest reviewed row but nothing before it. The procedure implies that R1 fails, and I read it that way, but R1's wording does not say so directly.
5. Which period's capital to show for a line whose latest row is not reviewed. The format's capital line does not say; I used the capital of the latest reviewed row and wrote the period.
6. The format's layout shows the latest-period row first, but a line with no reviewed latest row needs a different layout. The template gives the label ("NOT finance-reviewed") but not the order.
7. "What would change the placement" is asked for, but the template does not limit it to the failed tests. I added a speculative clause once (see above) and the example did not.
8. The format has a placeholder for the preparer's role and audience, and a closing line with "[role]'s review and [audience]'s discussion"; the template does not say what to do with the managing director, who is not in the roles list, when the settings name one.
9. The accounting-differences step covers "a basis_note that differs between lines for the same kind of figure". Revenue recognition differs among all four lines, and the trainer-pay note is a cost held elsewhere. I counted two differences, as the example did. The template does not say whether to count each pair of lines or one per kind of figure.

Shortcomings of the template found in this run:

- The acceptance tests are still marked "Proposed tests. None has been run." This trial is one run on one example; it does not replace them.
- The template is silent on how to word a question for the line owner where the template's role list has both the line owner and the finance lead involved (for example, the projects that slipped), so the split of such questions between roles is a judgment.
- The version note says "Not yet tried on its invented example." This trial is that attempt, with one model, on one run.

## Changes made after this run

After the review of this run, on the same day and without a rerun, the template, example and profile were revised. The output recorded above is the run's own and has not been altered. The changes:

- An untested estimate is now an amount, ratio or benefit offered as fact about the lines working together or about results; a plan (the three technicians) or a reason given for a change (the slipped projects) is not an estimate and is shown in the owner's account. This matches the example's count of two. Rerun on this version, the estimates would be two and the questions seven or eight; that count has not been checked by a run.
- R1 now says outright that both the latest and the prior period rows must be finance-reviewed, so Equipment rental fails it.
- The fit reading uses only the line's own owner's note.
- The template now says to show the capital of the row the measures come from and name its period when that is the prior period, and to show the latest period's Recorded row first.
- The example quotes the owner's words exactly, no longer gives capital as a balance at a date, gives the Equipment rental placement R3 as well as R1, and follows the output format's wording in its units line, its closing line and its placement note.

After the independent review, on the same day and again without a rerun, a second small round of edits was made. The output recorded above has not been altered. The changes:

- The template now says that "what would change the placement" is limited to what would make the failed tests pass, with no other scenario (ambiguity 7); that a person named in the settings but not in Roles, such as the managing director, is named in the Sources line and given no role (ambiguity 8); and that an accounting difference is counted once for each kind of figure, not once for each pair of lines (ambiguity 9).
- The instruction block now also carries the rules the procedure states: stop when no row is finance-reviewed, decline tax or legal advice, margin "not stated" when revenue is zero, a prior result of zero or below is not a fall, and "none stated" for accounting differences. It also has the line telling the reader to paste the output format after it, and two over-long lines are rewrapped.
- The example's Equipment rental Recorded rows were reordered to show the latest period first (the example originally had the prior row first, as recorded in the differences above). The example's Maintenance contracts placement now says both periods are finance-reviewed, the "strategy:" prefix on the fit quotes is dropped, the Head of projects question no longer includes the forecast part, which is now a separate question to the finance lead (the example has eight open questions, numbered anew), and the Equipment rental question also asks for the 2027 forecast to be reviewed.
- The example's inputs are unchanged and no rerun was made; the template was not changed in any step that alters the figures or placements. A rerun on this version has not been made.
