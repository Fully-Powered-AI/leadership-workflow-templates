# Business line investment review

For leadership with several business lines: each line set side by side on one basis (fit with strategy, finance-reviewed results, shared resources, open questions) and placed as invest, investigate or reduce commitment, for the leadership team to discuss and decide.

*Workflow template · Leadership · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/business-line-investment-review/)*

## The outcome

Leadership walks into the portfolio discussion with every line described the same way. Each line has the same blocks: how it fits the company's stated priorities, what it recorded in the same two periods, what it shares with the other lines, what its owner says, and what is still unanswered. Nobody argues from the line they know best.

Each line also arrives with a placement for discussion, invest, investigate or reduce commitment, and the plain rule that put it there. The rules are short and yours to change, so a disagreement is about the rule or the evidence, not about a hidden judgment. Where the numbers are not ready, the line says so and is placed as investigate with the question that would move it.

Leadership decides where to invest. The review makes no valuation, no sale or closure recommendation and no forecast of its own.

## Who starts the work, and when

The finance lead or the chief of staff starts it a week or two before a leadership session on where the company puts its money and attention, usually once or twice a year. The chief executive or owner reviews the draft with the finance lead, sends the open questions to line owners, and brings the approved version to the session.

## What they supply

- **The review settings.** The lines in scope with their owners, the two periods to compare, and the company's stated priorities, numbered.
- **A performance file** from finance: revenue, direct costs, result and capital used for each line and period, with a mark for whether finance has reviewed each row and a note on the basis it was prepared on.
- **Finance-reviewed forecasts,** if you have them, for the next period.
- **A shared-resource file:** each shared team, premises or system and the share of it each line uses.
- **A short note from each line owner,** in the owner's own words, including any benefit the owner expects from the lines working together.

## What comes out

One document with the same layout for every line: strategic fit with the words relied on, recorded performance for both periods with the measures worked out, change between periods, capital and return on capital used, the forecast as supplied, shared resources used, and the owner's account. After the lines: shared resources compared across them, untested estimates kept apart from the figures, accounting differences listed and left unadjusted, the placement of each line, and numbered open questions, each addressed to a role.

Nothing is averaged away. A figure finance has not reviewed is shown and labelled, never used. If the numbers do not agree with themselves, the review stops and says where.

It is separate from the annual operating budget, which sets next year's figures, from price change review, and from an operations KPI scorecard, which tracks measures of how the work runs. It compares lines with each other; it does not set a budget, a price or a target.

## What has to change for your company

Name your lines and their owners, and agree the two periods. Write your priorities as short numbered statements and say which lines each one names; the fit reading follows your words, not the assistant's view. Decide the fall in result that should stop a line being placed as invest, and which measure of capital to use, if any. Agree who at finance marks rows as reviewed, and what counts as the same accounting basis. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, from files and notes you supply; it needs no connection to your finance system.
