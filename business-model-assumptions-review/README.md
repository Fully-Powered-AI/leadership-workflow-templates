# Business model assumptions review

Before you commit money to a new or revised business model, see it block by block with every claim marked evidenced or assumed, the riskiest assumptions ranked, and the cheapest test for each. You choose what to test; no projection or valuation is made.

*Workflow template · Leadership · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/business-model-assumptions-review/)*

## The outcome

The sponsor sees the whole model on one page and can tell, claim by claim, what is known and what is hoped. The page lays out six blocks (customers, offer, channels, revenue, costs and partners), counts how much of each is backed by something that happened, and ranks the assumptions by how badly the model suffers if they are wrong and how unsure the sponsor is. For the riskiest few it names the cheapest way to find out.

A claim counts as evidenced only when money changed hands, people used the thing, or someone measured it, and the evidence has a date. A customer saying they like the idea, or an estimate, keeps the claim in the assumed column. That keeps a warm conversation from passing for proof.

Nothing is decided for you. The review ends with a choice line for each of the riskiest assumptions: test now, later, or accept as it is. It makes no sales forecast, margin figure or valuation, and it sets no pass or fail line for you.

## Who starts the work, and when

The founder, or the executive sponsor of a new line of business, starts it before spending serious money or time on a model: a new offer to the market, a change of price structure, a move from one-off sales to a subscription, a new customer group. It is worth running again whenever the model changes or a test comes back.

The sponsor writes a short brief and fills in a list of claims, one row each, with the evidence behind it and two ratings. The AI assistant lays out and ranks; the sponsor rates, chooses which assumptions to invest in testing and sets what result would count as a pass.

## What they supply

- **A short brief.** The model and what is changing, who sponsors it, the decision the review serves and when, the review date, and any context worth knowing.
- **The claims list.** One row per claim, filed under customers, offer, channels, revenue, costs or partners. Each row gives the kind of evidence (paid, used, measured, said, estimate or none), the evidence in a line, its date, and, for what is assumed, two ratings: how much the model suffers if the claim is wrong, and how unsure you are.
- **Test ideas, if you have them.** A test you already have in mind for a claim, with its cost and the days it takes.

## What comes out

One page in a fixed order. The model block by block, each claim marked evidenced or assumed; how many claims in each block are evidenced; the assumed claims in rank order; for the riskiest, the cheapest test, either yours or a proposal from a fixed ladder, with the pass and fail lines left empty for you; the evidenced claims whose proof has grown old; the questions only the sponsor can answer; and a choice line for each risky assumption.

Where something is missing, such as a block with no claims or an assumption nobody rated, the page says so and asks. It never fills the gap itself.

## What has to change for your company

Decide who sponsors: the person who decides where the money goes. Keep or change the two rating scales and the risk score at which an assumption counts as risky (6 by default), how many are shown as the riskiest (five), and how old evidence may be before it is rechecked (twelve months). Decide which kinds of evidence count; the default is paid, used or measured. Add your own default test for each block if the ladder does not suit how you sell. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from the brief and the list you type or paste; it needs no connection to any system.
