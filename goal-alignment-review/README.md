# Goal alignment review

Before next period's goals are set, the chief executive sees every team goal mapped to the company priority it serves: goals with no company parent, goals that pull against each other, priorities nobody owns, and the questions for each owner. It judges goals, never people.

*Workflow template · Leadership · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/goal-alignment-review/)*

## The outcome

The leadership session starts from one map instead of a stack of team plans. Every team goal sits under the company priority it claims, and each link is marked direct, when the goal moves the priority's own measure, or indirect, when the owner still has to show how it does.

The gaps that usually surface halfway through the quarter are on the page before it starts: a goal that answers to no priority, or to last quarter's list; a goal that needs another team's help that team has not planned; two goals that cannot both be met as written; a priority with no team goal under it, or no executive named to own it. Each arrives as a question with its facts, for the goal's owner or for the chief executive to settle with the department heads.

It describes goals, never the people who set them. Nothing rates a team or a manager, and nothing is sent to anyone.

## Who starts the work, and when

The chief of staff, the head of operations or whoever collects the goals runs it once teams have drafted them, about two weeks before the quarter, half or year begins and before the session that settles them. It runs again on the final goals to confirm every question was answered. The chief executive reads it, settles what pulls against what with the department heads, and approves the final goals.

## What they supply

- **The company priorities** for the period, each with its measure and the executive who owns it.
- **The team goals,** exported from the spreadsheet or goal-setting tool: each goal with its team, measure, target, owner, the priority it serves and any team whose help it needs.
- **Tensions already raised,** if anyone has said two goals may clash.
- **What the company is not doing** this period, if leadership has written it down.
- **A few settings:** the period, who decides, and whether teams may set a goal of their own.

## What comes out

One review, ready for the chief executive:

- a header that counts what was read, what was flagged and how many questions there are;
- the map: each priority with its measure, its owner and every goal under it, marked direct or indirect, then the goals teams set for themselves and the goals with no company parent;
- the goals flagged, each with every problem it has in one place;
- the goals that pull against each other, raised ones first, possible ones marked for both owners to confirm;
- the priorities nobody owns;
- the questions for each owner, grouped by owner, then the questions for the chief executive to settle with the department heads;
- anything that needs budget or people, pointed to the workflow template that handles it.

Every section says "none" when it has nothing in it.

## What has to change for your company

Map your goal-setting tool's columns to the default headers once and keep the mapping. Decide your period and how far ahead of it the review runs. Decide whether teams may set goals of their own and how many, and how many teams under one priority is worth a question. Write down what the company is not doing this period; the review is sharper with it. Name who decides, the chief executive alone or the leadership team together, and who shares the questions with the owners. It works with the money and the people left out: the budget and the headcount have their own workflow templates. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from the files you supply; it needs no connection to your goal-setting tool.
