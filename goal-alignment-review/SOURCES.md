# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. Three files of the strategic-alignment package in alirezarezvani/claude-skills (MIT) shaped the method. From the package's main instructions in c-level-advisor/skills/strategic-alignment: tracing each team goal up to the company goal above it and asking how closely the two are connected; the three problems worth looking for, namely a team goal with nothing above it, goals in different teams that work against each other, so that meeting one sets back the other, and a company goal no team has taken on; and giving every company goal a named owner. From c-level-advisor/skills/strategic-alignment/references/alignment-playbook.md: keeping a short list of what the company has decided not to do alongside its priorities, leaving room for a few goals teams set for themselves, naming a contributing owner when a goal is shared with a second team (the template's own needs_from check builds on this), and checking drafted goals shortly before the period opens and again once they are final. From c-level-advisor/skills/strategic-alignment/scripts/alignment_checker.py, read as text and never run: keeping tensions someone has already raised apart from ones that may exist, catching a goal that points to a company goal missing from the current list, and noticing a company goal that draws many teams while others go short, with the default of four or more teams for a busy priority. The post-acquisition use, when two sets of goals sit under one list of priorities, echoes the package's row for the period after a merger or acquisition. The package's scores, its test of asking staff to name the strategy, its analysis of silos and of how messages fade on their way down, its workshop agenda and its advice by company stage are not used. Fully Powered AI built the fixed export headers with a stop on any mismatch, the rule that a link is direct only when the goal counts the priority's own measure or part of it, the two rules for finding a possible tension from the goals' own measures and wording, the check that a team whose help a goal needs has planned it, the not-doing match marked for the owner to confirm, the company's own setting for the number of teams that makes a priority busy, one question per goal grouped by owner, the chief executive settling every tension and ownership gap with the department heads, money and people left to their own capabilities, owners named by role, and the rule that goals are judged, never people, and nothing is sent. No third-party text is reused.
