# Leadership meeting rhythm

Your chief executive gets a proposed calendar of leadership forums and written reports, built from today's meetings and the decisions left waiting: each forum with its purpose, inputs, attendees, frequency and one decision owner, and the hours it gives back. The chief executive chooses; no calendar is changed.

*Workflow template · Leadership · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/leadership-meeting-rhythm/)*

## The outcome

The week stops being a run of meetings that each end with "let's pick this up next time". Every recurring meeting the leadership sits in is looked at on its own figures: how often it meets, who is in the room, who owns its decisions, how many decisions it actually recorded, and whether anyone read anything before it started. Each one gets a single proposal. Keep it, sharpen it, fold it into a meeting that already covers the same ground, or let a short written report do its job.

What comes back is a rhythm the chief executive can see whole: a handful of forums, each with a purpose, the papers it needs, the people it needs, how often it meets and the one person who decides in it, plus the written reports that carry the updates so the time in the room goes on decisions. Every decision that has been waiting gets a home, and the ones the person closest to them can safely make on their own are handed back to them. The chief executive sees the hours they spend in recurring meetings today and under the proposal, against the ceiling they set.

## Who starts the work, and when

The chief executive or owner starts it, often with a chief of staff or executive assistant gathering the inputs. Good moments: when the calendar is full and decisions still wait; after the company grows, adds a leadership role or restructures; when a new chief executive inherits someone else's calendar; and once a year as a check.

The chief executive chooses, meeting by meeting, what to keep, change, merge or retire, and answers the few questions the proposal raises. A person then changes the invitations.

## What they supply

- **The meeting list:** each recurring internal meeting the leadership attends, with its purpose, day, frequency, length, attendees, chair, decision owner, how many decisions it recorded in the last 90 days and what papers go out before it. The template sets out default column headers; if a header differs, the assistant stops and asks.
- **The decisions waiting:** each decision raised and not yet made, with when and where it was raised, who can make it and whether it can be undone.
- **The reports that already exist,** such as a weekly dashboard or the monthly accounts.
- **A few settings:** today's date, the leadership team, who keeps the record, and the ceiling on the chief executive's meeting hours.

No calendar, mailbox or meeting tool is connected. Everything is pasted in by a person.

## What comes out

1. **The hours,** for the chief executive and for everyone, today and under the proposal, against the ceiling.
2. **Questions for the chief executive first,** such as a forum with nobody named to decide.
3. **Each current meeting** with its findings and one proposed choice.
4. **The proposed forums,** each with purpose, what it decides, inputs, attendees, frequency and length, decision owner, and the waiting decisions it takes first.
5. **The proposed written reports,** with owner, readers, contents and what each replaces.
6. **Every waiting decision** with how long it has waited and where it goes.
7. **The calendar changes** a person would make once the chief executive has chosen.

## What has to change for your company

Set the chief executive's ceiling on recurring meeting hours, and decide what counts as a recorded decision: a line in the meeting notes, an entry in your decision log, or both. Name the leadership team and the person who keeps the record and makes calendar changes. Adjust the defaults if they do not suit you: how few decisions make a meeting worth rethinking, how long a decision may wait before it is marked overdue, how many people a deciding forum may hold, and when the weekly leadership report goes out. Say which meetings are never redesigned, such as the board meeting. Keep the default column headers or tell the assistant yours. It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from the lists you paste in.
