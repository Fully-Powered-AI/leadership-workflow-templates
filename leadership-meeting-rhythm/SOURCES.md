# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. One open-source package informed the method: the operating-cadence package in beingsmit/technical-product-gtm (MIT). From it come the habit of telling a company's meetings apart by what each is for, how often it sits and who decides in it; the view that a recurring meeting which settles nothing should either move into writing or be rebuilt around the decisions it ought to take, with a smaller room; one named person who decides in each forum; letting the owner of a decision that can be undone make it without waiting for a meeting; a leadership forum every second week, with the updates read beforehand; and taking meetings away rather than adding them as a company grows. Its weekly metrics format, quarterly planning cycle, chief executive's weekly note and company-size tiers are not used. The meeting-list and waiting-decisions exports with a stop on any header mismatch, the yearly hours count for the chief executive and for everyone, with the chief executive's weekly hours set against a ceiling, the findings and the order in which proposals are chosen, the overlap and few-decisions rules, the routing of each waiting decision with its days waiting, the rule that no decision owner is ever chosen by the assistant, the list of calendar changes for a person to make and the acceptance tests are Fully Powered AI's own. No third-party text is reused.
